# CONP Ethics Toolkit

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.5167947.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5167947)

In response to a growing need in the neuroscience community for concrete guidance on ethically sound and pragmatically feasible open data-sharing, the Canadian Open Neuroscience Platform (CONP) has created an Ethics Toolkit. It helps researchers identify the elements of their project design that are often required for the open sharing of neuroscience data, such as model consent language and approaches to de-identification.

The toolkit consists of two guides:

* **The CONP Consent Guide**: model prospective consent clauses and a retrospective consent filter. [Read online](https://github.com/CONP-PCNO/Ethics_and_Data_Governance/blob/main/CONP%20Consent%20Toolkit.md) · [PDF](https://github.com/CONP-PCNO/Ethics_and_Data_Governance/blob/main/CONP_Consent_Guide_v1.0.5.pdf)
* **The CONP Privacy and De-identification Guide**: de-identification information and tools. [Read online](https://github.com/CONP-PCNO/Ethics_and_Data_Governance/blob/main/CONP%20De-Identification%20Toolkit.md) · [PDF](https://github.com/CONP-PCNO/Ethics_and_Data_Governance/blob/main/CONP_De-identification_Guide_v1.0.5.pdf)

This guidance is the product of extended discussions and careful drafting by the CONP Ethics & Data Governance Committee, and considers both Canadian and international ethical frameworks and research practice. It was drafted under Bartha Maria Knoppers (Chair) and Michael Beauvais (Manager), with Ann Cavoukian, John Clarkson, Lindsay Green-Noble, Judy Illes, Jason Karamchandani, Roland Nadler, Dylan Roskams-Edris, and Walter Stewart, and revised under Trudo Lemmens (Chair) and Alexander Bernier (Manager), with Patrick Bermudez. Further feedback was graciously provided from members of the CONP community, in particular Marcel Farrés Franch, Jessica Royer, and Robert Zatorre.
